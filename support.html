Getting started

Open Blabacus and press Start (Space). Allow microphone access when macOS asks. As people talk, a rod (abacus) or slice (pie) appears for each voice. Pause (Space) stops listening, Stop (⌘.) ends the conversation, and Clear (⌘K) starts over.

The first launch is slower

The first time, macOS compiles the speech models for the Neural Engine. Blabacus is usable within about 10 seconds and switches to the Neural Engine automatically when it is ready. Later launches are instant.

Microphone access is off

Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Microphone, and turn on Blabacus.

It merged two people, or split one person in two

Very similar voices, distance from the Mac, and noise make speakers harder to tell apart. Sit closer to the Mac and press Clear to start fresh. Naming people (click their rod or name) helps Blabacus recognize them next time. Blabacus can show up to 8 voices and is most accurate with two to four people. In a room with a distant microphone, a quiet participant may appear late, or not at all.

Why is the highlight a few seconds behind?

Blabacus highlights a speaker only once it is sure who was talking, about 4 seconds after the words. That way it matches the percentages and does not flicker. The percentages update continuously, and a new person appears after about 10 seconds of their speech.

What Mac do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon and 16 GB of memory, on macOS 26 or later. Blabacus still runs with less memory, but says so in About, and it may fall behind when other apps are busy.

What counts as a monologue?

Someone holds the floor for 45 seconds while others only say short things like "yeah" or "right". It ends when someone else takes a real turn (3 seconds or more), when the speaker pauses for 6 seconds, or when the conversation turns into back-and-forth.

Mini View

Minimizing the window shows a small floating Mini View. Hover over it to open the full window again or to hide it to the menu bar. Turn this off in Settings with "Minimize to Mini View".
